# Qualification and Risk Policy

The governing constraint on this programme: a link that carries penalty risk is worse than no link. Growth never justifies exposure.

Re-verify this document against Google's published spam policies quarterly. Search enforcement changed materially through 2025 and 2026 and this file is a snapshot, not scripture.

## Google's position, current

Three policies matter to link building.

**Link spam.** Any link intended to manipulate ranking, rather than earned or editorially placed, is in scope. That explicitly includes buying or selling links for ranking purposes, excessive link exchanges, large-scale guest posting with keyword-rich anchors, requiring a link as a term of service, and automated link creation. Paid links are permitted commercially, but must be marked `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`, which also means they pass no ranking value. A vendor selling a "dofollow guaranteed" placement is selling a policy breach.

**Site reputation abuse.** Publishing third-party content on an authoritative domain primarily to exploit that domain's ranking signals. This is what the industry calls parasite SEO. Enforcement has been active and expanding, and the sites hosting such content have been penalised, taking the placements with them. Treat any "publish on a major news domain for a fee" offer as high risk to us and terminal for the host.

**Scaled content abuse.** Mass-produced content, whether machine or human generated, made primarily to manipulate ranking. Relevant because it describes most of the syndication and guest-post-farm ecosystem.

## Automatic rejections

Reject on sight. No escalation, no exceptions.

- Any site with a published price list for links or "sponsored posts" that will be followed
- Private blog network footprints: near-identical templates across several domains, shared hosting patterns, the same outbound link neighbourhoods, no real authorship
- Sites whose content is overwhelmingly guest contributions with no editorial identity of their own
- Link exchange schemes, link wheels, reciprocal-link directories
- Comment sections, unmoderated forums, wiki spam, profile-link farms
- Sites with outbound link neighbourhoods in gambling, adult, pharmaceutical or crypto pump content, regardless of the page we would appear on
- Sites publishing at high volume with no discernible traffic or audience
- Machine-generated or scraped content sites
- Anything offering a guaranteed followed link for money

## Escalate to the director, do not decide alone

- A relevant, real publication that charges a fee for a clearly labelled sponsored placement. This is legitimate advertising and may be worth buying for referral traffic and brand, but it passes no ranking value and must be marked. The decision is commercial, not SEO.
- Press release distribution. Syndication produces many near-duplicate copies that Google discounts heavily. It can be worth buying for brand mentions, which do drive AI citation, but it should never be sold internally as a backlink tactic.
- Any outreach to an existing client, partner, supplier or investor, which must route through the relationship owner.
- Reviving a prospect that previously declined.

## The five qualification axes

Every prospect is screened on all five. Record the evidence, not just the verdict.

1. **Risk** - against the rejection list above
2. **Relevance** - topical fit to the client's actual services, and geographic fit to its market. In-country trade relevance beats global generic authority for a local business.
3. **Reality** - recent dated posts, named authors with histories, coherent publication identity, some sign of an audience
4. **Dedupe** - not already linking to the client, not pitched in the last 6 months unless a genuinely different tactic on a different page
5. **Reachability** - a plausible route to a named human or a stated submission process

## Tiers and honest conversion expectations

| Tier | Description | Expected conversion |
|------|-------------|--------------------|
| A | Relevant, real, editorial, demonstrated route in. Reclamation and warm relationship prospects. | ~1 in 3 |
| B | Relevant and real, needs a persuasive pitch plus an asset. | ~1 in 10 |
| C | Mechanical completions: directories, profiles, listings. Mostly nofollow. | Near 100% completion, low value |

Plan pipeline volume from these numbers. For 4 verified links a month, source 35 to 50 prospects. Inflating a tier to make a target look reachable is the most common self-inflicted failure in link building.

## Portfolio governance thresholds

| Signal | Threshold | Action |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Exact-match commercial anchors | Above ~10% of followed links | Vary anchors in upcoming outreach; request branded or naked URL anchors |
| New referring domains in a month | Above ~3x trailing average | Investigate; if a planned PR push, annotate as explained |
| Followed links into 404s | Any | Fix with a redirect on our own site immediately; needs no outreach |
| Confirmed links lost | Above ~10% in a quarter | Investigate cause; consider recovery outreach on the valuable ones |
| Single tactic share of pipeline | Above 50% | Diversify; a monoculture pipeline collapses when that channel closes |

## Disavow

Google ignores the overwhelming majority of spam links automatically. A disavow file is a power tool that mostly does harm in inexperienced hands. Recommend one only where there is a demonstrable adversarial pattern at scale, and say plainly that routine hygiene disavowing is not advisable.

## Anchor text policy for outreach

Ask for branded anchors or a naked URL by default. Never request an exact-match commercial phrase. Never supply pre-written anchor text as a condition. If a publisher offers to use whatever anchor we want, ask for the brand name, because a natural profile is dominated by brand and URL anchors and that is what we are trying to build.
