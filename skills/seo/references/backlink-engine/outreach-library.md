# Outreach Library

Templates per tactic, plus the follow-up cadence. Every template is a starting structure, never a send-as-is. A draft that could be sent to two different prospects unchanged has failed its personalisation requirement.

## Universal rules

1. **One verifiable specific about their page** in the opening. Prove a human read it.
2. **The ask is one sentence and it is small.**
3. **A reason that serves their reader.** Never say the link helps our SEO. Saying it out loud loses the link.
4. **Under 150 words** for reclamation and repairs. Under 200 for a credibility pitch.
5. **No flattery, no fake urgency, no guilt.**
6. **Real signature** with actual name, role, company.
7. **Follow their stated process** where they publish one, and say you have.
8. **Drafts only.** A human sends every email.

## Punctuation rule, non-negotiable

**No em dashes or en dashes anywhere in a drafted email.** Not in the subject, not in the body, not in the signature. Use a hyphen, a comma, a colon, or split the sentence.

This is a hard rule, not a style preference. Em dashes are one of the strongest tells that a message was machine written, and a publisher who reads outreach as AI-generated deletes it. It also has to survive the client's own voice guide: if a brand style guide calls for em dashes, that guide does not apply to outreach drafted by this system.

Before any draft is created, re-read the body and strip every em dash (U+2014) and en dash (U+2013). Where a dash was carrying a pause, a comma or a full stop reads better anyway.

## Subject lines

Short, specific, no marketing tone. Good: "Quick fix on your MVP Ventures piece". "Jay's episode page, small link request". "Correction on your 2026 roundup". Bad: anything with "Collaboration Opportunity", "Quick question", "Partnership", or an emoji.

## 1. Unlinked mention reclamation

```
Subject: Quick fix on your [ARTICLE TOPIC] piece

Hi [NAME],

You featured [BRAND / PERSON] in [EXACT ARTICLE TITLE] back in
[MONTH YEAR]. [ONE SPECIFIC, TRUE OBSERVATION ABOUT THE PIECE.]

One small thing: the piece mentions [BRAND] but does not link to
us, so readers who want to follow up have to go and search for the
name. Would you be open to linking the first mention to
[HOMEPAGE URL]?

Happy to supply an updated one-line company description if that
helps you refresh the page at the same time.

Thanks,
[SIGNATURE]
```

## 2. Redirect-only link repair

```
Subject: [PAGE TOPIC] page, small link request

Hi [NAME],

[PERSON] was on [SPECIFIC PAGE OR EPISODE] and we still point
people to it.

The links to us on that page currently route through
[REDIRECT DOMAIN]. Would you mind pointing them straight at
[TARGET URL] instead? Saves your readers a hop, and it means the
page shows up properly when people search for [PERSON OR BRAND].

Anything you need from us for the page, just say.

Thanks,
[SIGNATURE]
```

## 3. Roundup or resource page submission

```
Subject: [ROUNDUP TITLE], suggestion for the list

Hi [NAME],

Your [EXACT PAGE TITLE] lists [SPECIFIC CRITERION QUOTED FROM
THEIR PAGE]. [BRAND] fits that directly: [ONE SENTENCE OF
EVIDENCE AGAINST THEIR OWN CRITERION, WITH A REAL NUMBER OR FACT].

If it is useful, here is everything you would need in the format
your other entries use:

[NAME] - [ONE LINE, MATCHING THEIR EXISTING ENTRY LENGTH]
[URL]
[LOCATION]

No problem at all if it is not a fit.

Thanks,
[SIGNATURE]
```

## 4. Journalist or expert request response

Answer the question. Nothing else. No pitch, no attachment, no "let me know if you need anything else on other topics".

```
Subject: Re: [THEIR EXACT REQUEST HEADLINE]

Hi [NAME],

[DIRECT ANSWER TO THE QUESTION ASKED, 60 TO 120 WORDS, WRITTEN AS
A QUOTE THE JOURNALIST CAN LIFT VERBATIM. SPECIFIC, WITH A REAL
NUMBER OR A CONCRETE EXAMPLE. NO MARKETING LANGUAGE.]

Attribution if you use it:
[FULL NAME], [ROLE], [COMPANY] ([URL])

[ONE LINE OF CREDENTIAL EXPLAINING WHY THIS PERSON CAN SPEAK TO
IT.]

Happy to expand on any part of that before your deadline.

[SIGNATURE]
```

## 5. Podcast guest pitch

```
Subject: Guest idea: [SPECIFIC ANGLE, NOT THE COMPANY NAME]

Hi [NAME],

I look after marketing for [COMPANY]. Our [ROLE] [NAME] has done
this before, most recently on [PREVIOUS SHOW] talking [TOPIC], so
he turns up prepared and does not pitch on air.

One angle your audience would get value from: [SPECIFIC,
CONTRARIAN OR USEFUL THESIS IN ONE SENTENCE], and [WHAT LISTENERS
WOULD TAKE AWAY]. He can bring real numbers, including
[SPECIFIC ANONYMISABLE CLIENT OUTCOME].

Happy to send a two-line bio and a few sample questions if it is
worth a look.

Thanks,
[SIGNATURE]
```

## 6. Broken link replacement

Lead with the favour.

```
Subject: Broken link on your [PAGE TITLE] page

Hi [NAME],

Small heads-up: on [EXACT PAGE URL], the link to [DEAD RESOURCE
NAME] under [SECTION NAME] is returning a 404. Looks like they
took the page down some time in [PERIOD].

If you need a replacement for that spot, we published
[OUR RESOURCE] which covers the same ground: [URL]. Use it or
not, entirely up to you, I mostly wanted to flag the dead link.

Thanks,
[SIGNATURE]
```

## 7. Client, partner or supplier

Route through the relationship owner. Never cold-contact a client's marketing team.

```
Subject: Small favour on the [PROJECT] work

Hi [NAME],

Hope [SPECIFIC THING ABOUT THEIR BUSINESS] is going well since we
wrapped [PROJECT].

Two things, both small and both optional. We are building out the
case study on [PROJECT] and would love to include [SPECIFIC
DETAIL] if you are comfortable with it. And if you have a partners
or suppliers page, a listing for us there would genuinely help.

Happy to return the favour on our side, we would be glad to write
you a testimonial or list you as a partner too.

Thanks,
[SIGNATURE]
```

## 8. Testimonial for link

Very high conversion. Most vendors are actively looking for these.

```
Subject: Happy to give you a testimonial for [PRODUCT]

Hi [NAME],

We have used [PRODUCT] for [TIME PERIOD] at [COMPANY] and it has
genuinely [SPECIFIC OUTCOME].

If it is useful for your site, I am happy to write a short
testimonial or do a customer story. Just let me know the format
you prefer and who to send it to.

Thanks,
[SIGNATURE]
```

## Follow-up cadence

Draft the whole sequence at the same time as the first email and label each with its send date. **Most links come from the second touch**, so a programme that skips follow-up leaves roughly half its links on the table.

**Follow-up 1, five working days after the initial.** Short. Adds a new piece of information rather than repeating the ask.

```
Hi [NAME],

Following up on the below in case it slipped through.

[ONE NEW USEFUL THING: A RELEVANT STAT, A SECOND SUGGESTION, AN
OFFER TO SUPPLY SOMETHING IN THEIR PREFERRED FORMAT.]

Either way, no problem at all.

[SIGNATURE]
```

**Follow-up 2, ten working days after follow-up 1. Final.** Closes politely and leaves the door open.

```
Hi [NAME],

Last note from me on this, I do not want to clutter your inbox.

If [THE ASK] is not something you can do, completely understood.
If it becomes relevant later, [SPECIFIC OFFER: WE HAVE DATA ON
X, HAPPY TO COMMENT ON Y ANY TIME].

Thanks for the [ORIGINAL COVERAGE / THE PAGE], either way.

[SIGNATURE]
```

**Never a third follow-up. Never guilt. Never "just bumping this to the top of your inbox."**

## Contact form copy

Where the only route is a form, produce paste-ready text rather than an email. Compress to the form's field limits, drop the greeting and signature if there are separate name and email fields, and lead with the single most specific sentence.

## Tracking

Log every send in the outreach ledger with prospect domain, contact, tactic, send date, follow-up dates and outcome. The reply-to-link conversion rate per tactic is the number that tells the director which tactics to expand next cycle, and it is only knowable if every send is logged.
